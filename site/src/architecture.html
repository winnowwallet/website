01 / Find your transactions

Small summaries.
Local decisions.

Winnow downloads compact block filters from Bitcoin peers and checks them on your phone. If a filter may match your wallet, the phone fetches the full block to look for the transaction.

Your address watchlist stays local. Peers still see network requests, and false matches mean some downloaded blocks contain nothing for you.

While Receive is open, Winnow listens for relayed transactions to help you notice incoming payments before they confirm. Block scanning then finds confirmed payments.

02 / Keep what you found

Recovery starts
with your history.

A key tells the wallet what it can spend. Saved wallet history tells it what it has already found.

The encrypted iCloud backup contains both, along with account information and saved labels. Restoring a successful backup avoids rediscovering everything it already recorded. The phone still catches up on activity since that backup.

The backup encryption key syncs separately through iCloud Keychain. Other people’s private signing keys are not part of your backup.

03 / Agree, then sign

The account sets
the rules.

A shared account describes which keys are needed. Each signer reviews and signs the same transaction. A partially signed Bitcoin transaction—PSBT—carries the work between signers.

No Winnow coordination server is required. The account can use a normal approval threshold and an optional delayed recovery path. Those are separate spending rules.

04 / From source to screen

Lean at the core.
Native on the phone.

Wallet logic, Bitcoin cryptography and app state are written in Lean. Lean compiles to C. A generated Objective-C shell connects the app to Apple’s native controls.
