Winnow checks Bitcoin data on your phone instead of sending an address watchlist to a wallet-history service.

Your address list stays with you. The sections below explain the information used by peers, backups and shared accounts.

01 / On your phone

Your keys.
Your wallet state.

Your phone keeps its signing key in the device Keychain. Winnow asks for device authentication before reading it. Wallet history, accounts and saved labels are local app data.

The published privacy policy states that the app has no analytics, advertising SDKs or app crash-reporting service. Winnow does not require a user account or a wallet-history backend.

Receive labels are not added to an address or QR code. They can still be included in your encrypted cloud backup.

02 / On the network

Your connection.
Your choice.

Bitcoin peers can observe your IP address, connection timing, requested blocks and relayed transactions. Signed transactions become public on Bitcoin.

Opening a block explorer shares the transaction identifier and your connection information with that service. Your own Tor or I2P gateway can see connection metadata too.

Winnow does not bundle Tor, I2P, a VPN or Tailscale. Automatic gateway routing can fall back to a direct connection if no gateway is available; settings can require a gateway.

03 / In your backup

Encrypted before
it leaves your phone.

Automatic iCloud backup is on by default. Winnow encrypts the phone’s key, saved wallet history, accounts, public signer details, people and labels before upload.

The separate decryption key syncs through end-to-end encrypted iCloud Keychain. Access to both the backup and that key can restore the phone’s spending access. Apple can still see metadata such as file size, save times and connection information.

Turning backup off, or deleting the local wallet, does not delete existing copies in iCloud.

04 / With other signers

Shared accounts.
Shared information.

Co-signers need public account information to participate in the same wallet. That information can expose balances and transaction history even though it cannot sign by itself.

The published roadmap identifies a current limitation: the phone’s shared-account key is also its everyday BIP86 account key. Sharing it can let co-signers see that account’s history. New per-person payment cards carry neither wallet key; older cards can still be read.

Choose the people who will participate and keep each signer’s key and backup arrangements clear.

05 / Your choices

Know what
you are sharing.

Protect exported files even when they contain no recovery phrase: they can reveal private wallet history. The default manual file is watch-only; including the recovery phrase is an explicit additional choice.

Check the routing setting before relying on a gateway. Keep a recovery copy you understand, and make sure you can access the Apple Account and Keychain needed for iCloud recovery.

Visiting the website also creates ordinary web request metadata. App privacy and website hosting are separate contexts.
