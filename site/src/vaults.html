01 / Build the account

Start with the people.
Then choose the rule.

Save the signers you want to use: this phone, a supported Ledger, public keys someone shares with you, or an existing account.

A member can contain more than one key. For example, your phone and Ledger can jointly form your one signing role, with both keys required for that role.

Choose the required number of members, then share the public account descriptor so the other signers can join the same account. Each signer keeps their own private keys.

In an any-2-of-3 account, any pair can approve—including a trustee and provider together, without your signature. Choose the members to match the approval arrangement you want.

02 / A separate recovery rule

Set the wait.
Know when it starts.

You can configure a recovery path with fewer signatures or a separate recovery key. Set a delay from about 1 to 455 days, expressed in Bitcoin blocks.

StepWhat happens
1. A coin confirmsIts delay begins.
2. The blocks passThe recovery path becomes available for that coin.
3. Recovery keys signThe chosen threshold still has to be met.

Each coin’s delay starts at its confirmation and is measured in Bitcoin blocks. Days are approximate. The normal approval path remains available throughout.

03 / A plan for your family

Make access
understandable.

A recovery policy can be part of a plan for the people you leave behind. They need to know which keys they will need, where the account information is kept and what the time condition means.

Bitcoin follows the signatures and time conditions you set. Combine that account rule with clear instructions and your wider family or estate plan.

Professional trustee and inheritance services are on our roadmap.

04 / Preserve the whole account

More than
a recovery phrase.

Keep the account descriptor as well as the backups for your keys. A seed phrase alone does not describe a multisig account or its other signers.

The default manual backup file is watch-only. Including the recovery phrase is a separate choice that also enables restoring the phone’s signing key. Each other signer remains responsible for their own key.

The privacy page explains which account information is shared with co-signers, including how the current phone key relates to the everyday account.
