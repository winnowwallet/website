How it works

A light wallet.
A fuller backup.

Connect directly. Restore your history. Add independent signing protection. The useful ideas, and the details behind them.

01 / The connection

Your phone does
the matching.

Winnow connects to Bitcoin peers. It uses compact block filters: small summaries that help it find blocks that may contain your transactions. Your phone checks the filters locally, then downloads the relevant blocks.

An address-lookup service needs to know which addresses you want it to check. Local filtering avoids sending that service your address list. You don’t need to operate a desktop or your own Bitcoin node.

As a light client, Winnow works with Bitcoin peers to find your transactions without storing the whole blockchain on your phone.

Read the BIP157 protocol and BIP158 filter format.

02 / The backup

Your keys.
Your history, too.

A recovery phrase restores keys. It does not contain the history your wallet has already discovered. Without that history, a wallet may need to scan past blocks again, or ask an address-lookup service to find its transactions.

Winnow’s backup includes the phone’s signing key and saved wallet history, plus saved people and labels. Restoring that state lets it continue from the backup rather than rediscover everything it already knew. It still needs to catch up on activity since the backup.

Winnow encrypts the backup before storing it in iCloud. Its decryption key syncs through end-to-end encrypted iCloud Keychain. Recovery requires a successful backup and access to the required Apple Account and Keychain. It does not replace a missing hardware key or another member’s key.

Inspect the backup implementation.

03 / Network privacy

Your addresses.
Matched locally.

Filters can return false matches, causing extra block downloads. They also use more bandwidth than an address-lookup service. Downloads vary with wallet activity, catching up and recovery.

Local filtering keeps your address watchlist on the phone. Bitcoin transactions remain public, and peers can see your connection and block requests. Your routing choices are explained on the privacy page.

Saved wallet history lets a restored wallet pick up from its backup. It can continue checking locally rather than ask a service to look up its addresses.

See Bitcoin Optech’s overview and BIP157’s client recommendations.

04 / Independent signing

Two keys.
Different code.

Two devices can hold separate keys and still share a weakness if their signing software depends on the same vulnerable code. Winnow’s Lean wallet implements Bitcoin signing independently. Paired with hardware that uses a different implementation, this can reduce exposure to a shared cryptographic software flaw.

Both devices follow Bitcoin’s signing rules and use its secp256k1 curve. The difference is in the code that performs the signing.

Choose an account that requires both signatures, so the phone and hardware each provide their own approval.

See the open Lean source. The signing implementation and its Lean proofs are available to explore.

05 / Shared approval

Any two means
any two.

In the illustrated setup, the three members are you, a trustee and a provider. Your phone and hardware together form your approval. The trustee and provider each hold their own signing key.

Any two members can approve: you + trustee, you + provider, or trustee + provider. The trustee and provider can therefore approve together without your signature. Each member has a clear role in the account’s rule.

An optional delayed recovery path adds another way to spend; it does not prevent a valid pair from using the normal path. Its delay is measured from each coin’s confirmation, not a report that a device is missing or an owner has died. The chosen recovery keys and threshold determine who can use it.

Choose the approval rule around the people you want to involve. For a family recovery plan, record which keys they will need and how to access the account information.

Explore the account creation and recovery rules.

Why “Winnow”?

Winnowing means separating grain from the husks around it—keeping the useful part.

Winnow brings that idea to Bitcoin. Your phone checks small summaries to find the blocks that may contain your transactions, instead of downloading the whole blockchain.
